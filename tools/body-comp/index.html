How the Navy tape method works

The US Navy method estimates body fat from a few tape measurements and your height. For men it uses neck and waist. For women it adds the hips. It was built by Hodgdon and Beckett for military fitness testing, and for most people it lands within about 3 to 4 percentage points of a lab scan. That is plenty accurate to track a trend, which is what actually matters.

Measure in the morning, relaxed, tape snug but not pressing into the skin. Neck just below the voice box. Waist at the navel. Hips at the widest point. Same spots, same time of day, every time.

The scale is a tool, not a verdict

The number on the scale is a measurement. It is not your worth, and it is not even a direct read on fat. Water, salt, a hard training day and your cycle all move it. That is why we read the scale as a two-week trend and never as a single morning.

If you would rather not weigh at all, that is okay. Replace the data instead of removing it: measure your waist, arm, thigh and neck once a month. Just know what skipping the scale costs you, which is time. Fewer data points means it takes longer to tell whether something is working.

Why your waist is the read

Of all the measurements, your waist at the navel is the fat signal. If it is coming down, you are losing fat. Arms and thighs are mixed tissue, and they can hold steady or even grow as you build muscle. Waist down with arms and thighs holding is recomposition: losing fat and building muscle at the same time. It is the best outcome there is, and it is the one the scale is worst at showing.

Ideal weight and BMI, in context

Your ideal weight here uses the Hamwi formula, a simple estimate from height. It is a reasonable place to set a first goal, and you can move it as you learn your body. BMI is shown only as context. It divides weight by height and cannot tell muscle from fat, so a strong, muscular person can read as overweight. Use it as one more data point, never as a verdict.

Questions people ask

How accurate is the Navy body fat method?

For most people it lands within about 3 to 4 percentage points of a lab scan like DEXA. It is best used to track your own trend over time with the same tape, spots and time of day.

Where do I measure my waist?

At the navel, standing relaxed, after a normal breath out. For the neck, just below the voice box. For hips, at the widest point.

What is a healthy body fat percentage?

Common reference charts put fitness ranges around 21 to 24 percent for women and 14 to 17 percent for men, with average ranges a little above that. Treat them as context. Your trend matters more than any one reading.

Is BMI useful?

As context only. BMI cannot tell muscle from fat, so it misreads a lot of strong people. Your waist measurement and your trend over time tell you more.

What ideal weight formula do you use?

The Hamwi formula: 100 pounds for the first 5 feet plus 5 pounds per inch for women, and 106 plus 6 per inch for men. It is a starting point for a goal, not a rule.
