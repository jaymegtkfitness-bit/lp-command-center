What counts as a hard set

A hard set is a working set taken close to failure, usually with 1 to 3 good reps left in the tank. Warm-ups do not count. Neither do sets you stop halfway because they got uncomfortable. Research on muscle growth keeps landing on the same idea: the number of hard sets each muscle gets per week is one of the strongest levers you control.

How many sets you need

  • Beginner, about 10 sets per muscle per week. Almost anything works when you are new. More volume just adds soreness.
  • Intermediate, about 10 to 15. You need a little more stimulus to keep progressing.
  • Advanced, about 15 to 20. Only if you are recovering well and still adding weight or reps.

Those numbers are for the big groups: chest, back, quads, and hamstrings and glutes. Shoulders, arms, calves and core sit lower, because every press, row, squat and hinge already trains them. A bench press set counts for chest, and it also works your triceps and front shoulders.

Twice a week beats once

Spreading a muscle's weekly sets across two sessions tends to work better than cramming them into one. That is why two or three days of full body work so well, and why four days usually becomes an upper and lower split run twice. Five and six days just give you more room, not more magic.

In a fat-loss phase, hold the low end

When you are eating in a deficit, your recovery budget shrinks. The goal of training in a Lean phase is to keep the muscle you have, and that takes less volume than building it. Hold the low end of your range, keep the weights heavy, and let steps do the low-stress work.

More is not always better

Volume only helps as long as you can recover from it. If your lifts are stalling, your joints ache and your sleep is off, more sets will not fix it. Start in the middle of your range, add a set here and there while you are still progressing, and pull back when you are not.

Questions people ask

How many sets per muscle group per week should I do?

A good evidence-based starting point is about 10 hard sets per muscle per week for beginners, 10 to 15 for intermediates and 15 to 20 for advanced lifters, with smaller muscles a little lower.

What counts as a hard set?

A working set taken close to failure, usually with 1 to 3 reps left. Warm-up sets and easy sets do not count toward your weekly total.

Is it better to train a muscle once or twice a week?

Twice a week tends to work better for most people, because it spreads the weekly sets out and keeps each session productive.

Should I do less volume while losing fat?

Usually yes. In a deficit, recovery is limited, and the goal is to keep your muscle. Holding the low end of your range with heavy weights does that well.

What training split should I use?

Two or three days: full body. Four days: upper and lower, twice through. Five days: upper and lower plus a full body day. Six days: push, pull, legs, twice through.
