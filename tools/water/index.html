The rule: half your body weight in ounces

Take your body weight in pounds, cut it in half, and drink at least that many ounces of water a day. At 180 pounds, that is 90 ounces. It is simple enough to remember and close enough for almost everyone to start with. Think of it as a floor, not a ceiling. On hot days, sweaty training days or days you travel, you will need more.

Start with the morning

You wake up after seven or eight hours without a drink. Before coffee, before your phone, drink 16 to 20 ounces of water with a small pinch of salt. The salt adds a little sodium, which helps your body actually hold on to the water instead of sending it straight through. It is the easiest habit in this whole system, and it gets you roughly a quarter of the way to your number before breakfast.

One caution: if you manage blood pressure or your provider has you watching sodium, leave the salt out and ask them.

Why it matters more than you think

Mild dehydration shows up as things people rarely connect to water: low energy, a foggy afternoon, headaches, and hunger that is really thirst. It also makes your weigh-ins noisier, which makes your progress harder to read. Water is not a fat-loss thing or an energy thing. It is both, and so is everything else at this layer.

Your produce counts too

Fruits and vegetables are mostly water. If you are eating a solid amount of produce every day, it has been adding to your hydration the whole time. Keep the full water target anyway. The produce is just the reason it will feel easier than you expect.

How to actually hit it

  • Watch first. Log the ounces you actually drink for a few days before you change anything.
  • Use one bottle you know the size of, and count refills.
  • Drink most of it before dinner so it does not wake you up at night.

Questions people ask

How much water should I drink a day?

A simple starting point is half your body weight in ounces. At 180 pounds that is 90 ounces a day. Treat it as a minimum and drink more on hot or heavy training days.

Why add a pinch of salt to morning water?

A little sodium helps your body hold on to the water you drink after a night without any. Skip it if you manage blood pressure or your provider has you on a low-sodium plan.

Does coffee or tea count toward my water?

Count your water separately and let coffee and tea be a bonus. It keeps the habit simple and makes your number easy to track.

Does food count toward hydration?

Yes. Fruits and vegetables are mostly water, so a produce-heavy day helps. We still recommend hitting the full water number and treating food as extra.

Can I drink too much water?

For healthy adults it is very hard to drink too much at these amounts. Spread it through the day rather than drinking large amounts at once, and follow your provider if you have a heart or kidney condition.
