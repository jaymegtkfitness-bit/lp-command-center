Why protein is the anchor

If you only track one number, make it protein. It is the nutrient that protects your muscle when you are losing fat, the one that builds it when you are training, and the one that keeps you full on fewer calories. It also costs your body the most energy to digest, so eating enough of it quietly raises what you burn.

Most people we work with are not eating too much. They are eating too little protein and too much of everything that fills the gap. Fix the protein and a lot of the hunger, the cravings and the afternoon crash fixes itself.

Why goal weight, not current weight

Your protein is built from your goal weight. Extra body fat does not need extra protein, so using your current weight can push the number so high it becomes unrealistic. Anchoring to your goal feeds the body you are building, and the number stays steady as you get there.

How much by phase

  • Lean: 1 gram per pound of goal weight. A deficit is when muscle is most at risk, so protein goes highest.
  • Sustain: 0.9 grams per pound. Holding what you built.
  • Strong: 0.8 grams per pound. You are eating more calories overall, which also protects muscle, so the rate can come down a little.
  • Reverse: 1 gram per pound. Protein leads the climb, and calories rise as a result.

Split it across your meals

A daily number is easier to hit when every meal carries its share. Pick how you actually eat: two meals and a shake, three meals, three and a shake, or four. If you use a shake, its protein comes out first and the rest is split evenly across your meals. Aim to land each meal within 5 to 10 grams. You do not need to be perfect. You need to be close, most days.

Start by watching what you eat now for a week without changing anything. Then close the gap one meal at a time. Breakfast is usually the biggest opportunity.

Questions people ask

How much protein do I need a day?

We use your goal weight: 1 gram per pound in a fat-loss (Lean) phase, 0.9 in Sustain and 0.8 in a muscle-building (Strong) phase. A woman with a 145 pound goal eats 145 grams a day in Lean.

Should I use my current weight or my goal weight?

Your goal weight. Extra body fat does not need extra protein, and a number built on current weight is often too high to hit. Goal weight feeds the body you are building.

How much protein should I eat per meal?

Divide what is left after any protein shake by the number of meals you eat. On 145 grams with three meals and a 30 gram shake, that is about 38 grams per meal.

Is a protein shake necessary?

No. A shake is a convenient way to close the gap, not a requirement. If you use one, we reserve its protein before splitting the rest so your meals stay properly sized.

Can I eat too much protein?

For healthy adults, the ranges here are well within what research supports. If you have kidney disease or your provider has limited your protein, follow their guidance instead.
