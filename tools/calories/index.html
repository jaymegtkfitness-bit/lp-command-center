Why we anchor to your goal weight

Most calorie calculators start with the body you have right now. They take your current weight, run it through a formula, and hand you a number to eat less than. That works for a few weeks. Then the number that was built for a heavier body stops fitting, and nobody tells you what to do next.

We flip it. Your calories are built from your goal weight, the body you are working toward. Goal weight times 15 is maintenance for that body. So when you arrive, you are already eating like the person who lives there. There is no scary moment where the plan ends and you have to figure out maintenance on your own. You have been practicing it the whole time.

Maintenance is a range, not a number

Your real maintenance sits somewhere between your goal weight times 15 and your current weight times 15. Where you land depends on how much you move, how much muscle you carry, the quality of your food and a dozen other things no formula can see. We do not chase the exact number. We start inside the range, check in weekly, and let your own data move it.

The four phases

  • Lean (×13, then 12, then 11). Fat loss. You only step down when the scale has stopped setting new lows for long enough that it is not noise.
  • Strong (×15, then 16, then 17). Building muscle. You eat at or above maintenance so training has something to build with.
  • Sustain (×15). The hinge. Holding, recovering, and letting your body settle before the next season.
  • Reverse (×10, then 12, then 13). For anyone already eating at or under ten times their goal weight. You do not cut from there. You climb first.

What about the textbook formula?

Mifflin-St Jeor is a good formula, and we show it so you can compare. But it estimates your resting burn with an error of around ten percent, and the activity multiplier on top is mostly a guess. Two guesses stacked on the body you are trying to leave is not a better starting point. A simple anchor you can check against the scale every week is.

Whatever number you start with, it is a hypothesis. Protein first, consistency second, and two weeks of data before you change anything.

Questions people ask

How many calories do I need to maintain my weight?

A simple, reliable starting point is your goal weight times 15. Your true maintenance usually falls between goal weight times 15 and current weight times 15, depending on activity and muscle. Start in that range, weigh in three times a week, and adjust from your own trend.

Why use goal weight instead of current weight?

Because you eat for the body you are building. Anchoring to goal weight means that when you get there, you are already eating maintenance for that body, so there is no hard switch at the end.

What if I do not know my goal weight?

Leave it blank. We estimate an ideal weight from your height and sex using the Hamwi formula, which is a reasonable place to start. You can change it any time.

How is this different from Mifflin-St Jeor or a TDEE calculator?

Those estimate the calories your current body burns. We show that number as a compare line, but build your targets from your goal weight with a multiplier for each phase, and then let weekly check-ins adjust it.

When do I move from Lean step 1 to step 2?

Only when the scale stops setting new lows for about two weeks (about 10 days if you weigh daily) and you have been at least 85 percent consistent. A flat week is not a stall. A trend is.
