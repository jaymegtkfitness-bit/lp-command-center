One

There Are No Bad Foods

Only foods that cost more room than others. That is the whole idea.

Every diet you have tried worked by taking things away. No carbs. No sugar. No eating after seven. A list of villains, and a promise that if you could just avoid them, the weight would come off.

It is exhausting, and it is backwards. Food is not good or bad. A chicken breast and a ribeye are both just protein. The only real difference is how much room each one takes on your plate for the same job.

A power food is not a special food. It is a food that gives you the most for the least — the most protein, the most fiber, the most fullness, for the fewest calories.

That is all a "power food" means. It is the food that does the most work for the room it takes. Once you can see your food that way, you stop banning things and start spending your calories on purpose, the same way you spend money you actually respect.

Two

How The Tiers Work

Three tiers in every food group. It is a ranking, not a rulebook.

Every food group — your proteins, your carbs, your fats, your fruit — is sorted into three tiers. The tier is not about whether a food is allowed. It is about how much it gives you for the room it takes.

Tier 1 — build here first

The leanest, most filling, most nutrient-dense options. The most protein or the most fiber for the fewest calories. These are the foods that let you eat more actual food and still land on your numbers. Most of your plate, most of the time, comes from here.

Tier 2 — great, mind the portion

Excellent foods that carry a little more fat or a little less fiber. Salmon, steak, rice, quinoa, whole eggs, nut butters. Nothing wrong with a single one of them. You just watch the portion a touch more, because they take a bit more room.

Tier 3 — save room for these

The richest, most calorie-dense choices. Ribeye, oils, bagels, dark chocolate. Not banned, not bad — just expensive in room. You spend here on purpose, when the flavor is worth it, and you build the rest of the day to make space.

The rule in one line: build the plate on Tier 1, and step up to Tier 2 and Tier 3 on purpose, when you want the variety or the flavor.

That is the entire method. Not "never eat this." Just "know what everything costs, spend where it counts." You will never feel deprived, because nothing is forbidden. You are just building from the foods that give you the most freedom first.

Three

Vegetables Are Free

The one group that does not get counted.

Before the lists, the easiest win in all of nutrition. Non-starchy vegetables — leafy greens, broccoli, peppers, zucchini, cauliflower, asparagus, mushrooms, green beans, tomatoes, cucumber — are the foundation of every plate and you do not count them.

They are almost pure volume and fiber for almost no calories. They fill your plate and your stomach, they carry the nutrients a hard training block demands, and they make a lower-calorie day feel like enough food instead of a punishment. Pile them on. This is the one place where more is simply better.

Five

Build A Plate With It

Same order, every meal. It becomes automatic fast.

  1. Start with a Tier 1 protein. Pick it first and size it to your protein target. Protein is the hardest number to hit and the easiest to build a meal around. This is the anchor of the plate.
  2. Pile on the free vegetables. Volume before anything else. This is what makes the plate feel like plenty.
  3. Add a Tier 1 carb or fruit. Potatoes, rice, beans, berries, an apple. Real energy, and it keeps a training body fed.
  4. Finish with a thumb of fat. A little goes a long way. Tier 1 and Tier 2 fats first; the oils and butters are Tier 3 for a reason.
  5. Spend a Tier 2 or Tier 3 choice on purpose. Want the ribeye, the bagel, the square of dark chocolate? Build the rest of the day leaner and enjoy it with zero guilt. That is the system working exactly as designed.

Protein first, vegetables free, Tier 1 by default, everything else on purpose. That is a plate you can build for the rest of your life.
