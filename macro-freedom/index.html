Why eating intuitively fails when your intuition has been wrong for a decade.

You have been told to listen to your body.

Two

The Four Numbers

Everything runs on four starting points. You can write them on an index card.

Before the numbers, the thing that makes them work.

They are anchored to your goal weight, not your current weight.

That distinction sounds small and it is not. Anchoring to your current weight means your targets shrink every single time you succeed. You eat less and less to hold the same result, and you walk yourself into the exact hole most people are already standing in.

Anchoring to your goal weight means the number you start on is the number you keep. You feed the body you are building, not the one you are leaving.

CaloriesGoal weight × 13

The universal fat loss starting point. It is deliberately not aggressive, because there are two free meals living inside it.

Protein1 g per lb of goal weight

With an absolute floor at 0.8 g that you never go below, in any phase, ever. A floor, not a weekly average.

Produce600 to 800 g a day

Fruits and vegetables by weight. The simplest way to hit your fiber without ever thinking about fiber.

WaterHalf your bodyweight in oz

This one uses your current weight. Unglamorous, and the first thing that quietly wrecks a good week when it slips.

A worked example

A woman currently at 185 lbs whose goal weight is 160.

Calories 2,080
Protein target 160 g
Protein floor 128 g
Produce 600 to 800 g
Water 92 oz
Free meals 2 per week

That is the entire prescription. Not a list of banned foods, not a window she is allowed to eat in, not a rule about carbs after six.

All four are starting points, and that is not a hedge

They are accurate enough to work on day one for almost everybody, which is exactly what a starting point is for. They get sharpened later by your own data, and sharper still by bloodwork and genetics if you ever go that deep.

What they are not is a guess. Anyone who tells you they can hand you a perfect number before watching you eat for a week is selling you precision they do not have.

Three

The Week That
Changes Everything

Track honestly. Change nothing. This is the hardest week and the most valuable one.

Before you change anything, you look at it.

Seven days of honest tracking where you change nothing. Not one thing. You eat exactly how you already eat, and you write all of it down.

What "all of it" means

  • The oil in the pan, not just what is in the pan
  • The sauces, the dressings, the condiments
  • The wine, and the second glass
  • The handful of something on the way through the kitchen
  • The bites off someone else's plate
  • The chips and salsa before the food comes out
  • The creamer, every time, not once

Weigh what you can. A food scale for a week is not a life sentence, it is a calibration. After that week you will be able to eyeball portions for years.

Your job this week is not to perform. It is to observe.

Most people cannot do this, and that by itself tells you something worth knowing. The urge to clean it up the moment you start writing it down is the entire problem in miniature.

What the week is actually for

It is not to shame you. It is to sort you, because there are two completely different situations that produce the identical sentence, "I am eating 1,400 calories and nothing is happening."

If seven honest days land near 1,400, your metabolism has genuinely adapted down. You ate less and did more for years and your body lowered the floor to match. The answer is more food, not less. You walk the calories up, rebuild the engine, and build muscle so there is somewhere for the food to go.

If seven honest days land at 1,900 when you would have sworn it was 1,400, nothing is wrong with your metabolism. There were three to five hundred calories a day you genuinely could not see. The answer is learning to see them, which you just did.

Those two situations need opposite interventions, and getting it backwards is the one genuinely harmful mistake in all of this. Add food to someone who was already eating more than she thought and she gains, and you have confirmed the worst thing she believes about herself. Tell someone with an exhausted metabolism to try harder and you drive her further into the ground.

Nobody has to guess, and nobody has to be told they were wrong. Your own data settles it in seven days.

You are not lying to anyone

The chips before the meal. The wine with dinner. The bite off his plate. None of it registered as a decision, so none of it entered the math. That is not dishonesty. It is the most normal thing in the world, and nobody ever showed you how to look.

Four

Your Nine Meals

Build it once. Eat it ninety percent of the time. Stop deciding.

Off the back of that week, you build meals that hit your numbers and that you actually want to eat.

Three breakfasts. Three lunches. Three dinners. You rotate them.

Those nine become roughly ninety percent of your eating, which means ninety percent of your food decisions are already made before the week starts.

How to actually build them

  1. Start with the protein. Pick the protein first in every single meal and size it so the day lands on your target. Protein is the hardest number to hit and the easiest to fix by building around it instead of bolting it on afterward.
  2. Add the produce. Volume before anything else. This is where the 600 to 800 grams lives, and it is what makes a lower calorie day feel like enough food instead of a punishment.
  3. Fill the rest with what you like. Carbs and fats make up the remainder. There is no banned food here. If you want rice, eat rice. If you want bread, eat bread.
  4. Save them in your tracker. Every good tracker lets you save a meal. Do it once, then copy and paste. This is the step people skip, and it is the one that decides whether you are still doing this in March.

The other ten percent, the things outside the rotation, you do track. But you are tracking off the back of real awareness now instead of white knuckle logging, and it takes a fraction of the effort it used to.

You are not tired of eating well. You are tired of deciding.

You make a hundred decisions a day for people who count on you. What is for dinner, how much of it, whether a meal out ruined the week. Your food should not be another hundred decisions. Nine meals, built once, is how you stop.

Five

The Free Meals

Two a week. In the plan on purpose. Nothing to count and nothing to log.

Every plan you have ever run required a clean week to work.

There is no clean week. There is a birthday, a work dinner, a Friday you are too tired to cook, a holiday, a trip. So the plan has to work on your worst days, not just your best ones, or it is not a plan.

Which is why two free meals a week are built in from the start. They are not a cheat and they are not a reward for good behavior. They are structural.

A free meal is a meal where you eat pretty much whatever you want, and you do not track it.

Read that again, because the second half is the part nobody believes. You do not track it. There is no number attached to it, no logging it afterward, no doing math to see what it cost you.

What makes that safe is not arithmetic. It is what the rest of the day looks like.

The two rules, and they both live outside the meal

  1. Hit your protein floor from your other meals. All of it, before the free meal happens. If your floor is 120 grams, that is two meals at 50 grams each plus a protein shake, and you are already there. Then the free meal is genuinely free, because you were never counting on it for protein in the first place.
  2. Get your 600 grams of fruit and vegetables across those same meals. Same logic. The free meal is not where that comes from.

Those two rules are the whole mechanism. Meet them and the free meal takes care of itself. Miss them and no amount of careful eating at dinner fixes the day.

Three things inside the meal itself

  • A noticeable protein source. Not a token amount. Something you would point at and call the protein.
  • A fruit or a vegetable. One, and it does not have to be at the table. An apple in the car on the way to the restaurant counts. So does a bag of baby carrots before you leave the house. Getting it in early also makes the next rule a great deal easier.
  • Stop when you are 80 percent full. You are not going to engorge yourself. That is the only limit there is, and it is a feel, not a number.
The part nobody else will tell you

There is no number on a free meal.

I am not going to tell you what it is supposed to come to, because the moment I hand you a number for Thanksgiving dinner I have handed you one more thing to fail at.

You manage the composition of that plate, not the arithmetic of it. A protein. Something that grew. Eat what you actually want otherwise, and stop satisfied rather than stuffed.

You earned it from the rest of the day. That is the entire accounting.

Forecast on Sunday

Sit down with the week ahead and decide where your two are going. The work dinner on Thursday. Sunday lunch with your parents. Put them on the calendar.

This is the rule that changes December. A free meal you planned on Sunday is part of the system. The same meal, unplanned on a Thursday, is the thing that makes you write off the rest of the week.

Six

When The Scale Stalls

A rule instead of a feeling.

It will stall. That is not pessimism, it is arithmetic. Somewhere in the next few months there is a holiday, a trip, a stressful stretch, and a week where the number does not move.

If your only answer to a stall is to try harder, the first bad week ends the whole thing.

First, how to read the scale at all

Weigh three times a week, on the same three days, first thing, before you eat or drink. Then look at the line across two weeks, never a single morning.

One day is noise. Two weeks is a trend.

The scale is a tool, not a verdict. It is telling you what happened last week. It is not telling you who you are, and it is not a measure of whether you are a disciplined person.

Then, the three doors

  • Hold. One flat week is noise. You change nothing and you keep running. This is the correct answer far more often than people believe.
  • Adjust your portions. The trend has genuinely flattened across two weeks or more with your adherence intact. You make a small change, once, and you give it two more weeks.
  • Change your phase. You have been in a deficit long enough that a smaller number is no longer the answer. Fat loss is a season, not a permanent state, and the way out is up rather than further down.

Three doors. You pick one based on what the trend says, not on how you feel about yourself that morning. That is the entire difference between a system and a diet.

Seven

Your First Four Weeks

The order of operations. Do not skip ahead, and do not do two at once.

Week One

The read

Track everything, honestly, and change nothing. Weigh three times on the same three days. Do not adjust your eating, do not start a deficit, do not join a gym. Observe. At the end of the week you will know your real intake and which of the two situations you are in.

Week Two

Build your nine

Set your four numbers off your goal weight. Build three breakfasts, three lunches and three dinners that hit them, protein first, produce second. Save every one of them in your tracker. Eat them this week and adjust the ones you do not enjoy, because a meal you dislike will not survive a hard week.

Week Three

Run it, with the free meals in

Sunday, forecast your two free meals for the week ahead and put them on the calendar. Then run the rotation, and hit your protein floor and your produce from your structured meals every single day, so that when a free meal lands you have already met the day. Hit your water. Keep weighing three times.

Week Four

Read the trend

Look at the line across weeks three and four. Hold, adjust your portions, or change your phase. One decision, made from data, then back to week three.

After that you repeat weeks three and four, indefinitely. That is the whole loop, and it does not have an end date because it was never a diet.

If you only remember one thing

Do not start in week two. Everyone wants to skip the read and go straight to the numbers, and it is the single most common way this fails. Numbers built on a guess are just a more confident guess.
