WHY THIS WORKS

Three things decide whether you arrive lean or just arrive smaller.

Most people who lose weight on food alone give up a meaningful share of that loss as muscle. These three levers are what change that.

1

Hit your protein floor.

Protein is the loudest signal your body has for deciding what to keep when calories come down. Your floor is set from your goal weight and your phase, and it holds every single day. Not an average across the week. A floor.

2

Stop under-eating.

This is the one that surprises people. If you have been cutting for years, the answer may be to eat more on purpose before you ever cut again. Going lower is not always the way forward, and for some people it is the reason the scale stopped listening.

3

Lift, three days a week.

The single biggest lever outside the kitchen. Resistance training is how you tell your body the muscle is still needed. Muscle is the tissue that decides how you look, how you feel at 7pm, and how much you can carry into your sixties.

Why this matters past the mirror. Muscle mass is the asset that carries you through the decades after 30. Losing weight without protecting it leaves you lighter and weaker, with less of the tissue that drives how you look, feel, and function. Protecting it is not a bonus. It is the point.
ONE MORE THING

Everyone I have worked with fits one of three patterns.

After all these years, the people who come to me almost always land in one of these:

  1. Disciplined, for too long. Accurate, consistent, and dieting so long without a break that the body adapted. Doing everything right, and it stopped working.
  2. Close, but just missing. Doing most of it right, but the tracking is not quite as accurate as it feels, so the results never match the effort.
  3. Never taught the basics. No foundation, because nobody ever showed them what the basics actually look like.

None of the three is a willpower problem. Each one has a clear way forward, and your answers here tell me which one is yours.

10

What is going on in your body right now.

Answer these honestly. Almost none of them stop me from building your plan. What they do is change how it gets built, and the wrong plan for your situation is the thing I am actually trying to avoid.

Are you pregnant, breastfeeding, or trying to conceive?

This season needs fuel, not a deficit.

Then your plan will never be a deficit. It gets built at or above maintenance, protein-forward, with the produce and fiber targets that matter most right now. Take it to your OB or midwife before you start, and follow what they tell you over what I tell you.

Do you have diabetes, Type 1 or Type 2?

Carbohydrate amount and timing matter more for you than for most people.

Your plan gets built carbohydrate-aware: carbs spread evenly across meals instead of stacked into one, and built from the slowest-digesting Tier 1 sources. One thing you must do: eating differently can change what your medication needs to be. Tell your prescriber before you start.

Do you have kidney disease or reduced kidney function?

This system runs on a high protein floor, and that is the one thing reduced kidney function cannot safely absorb.

This is the one I will not work around. Protein is the engine of everything here, and setting it by your body weight instead of your kidney function is genuinely unsafe. You need a renal dietitian, not me. I will not charge you.

Do you have a diagnosed heart condition?

Heart disease, heart failure, an arrhythmia, a past heart attack, stents, or a pacemaker. Blood pressure alone is the next question.

Your plan gets built sodium-aware and leaning on Tier 1 fats, which are the unsaturated ones. Clear it with your cardiologist before you start, especially anything that changes how you train.

Do you have high blood pressure, or take blood-pressure medication?

Common and very workable.

Noted. Sodium-aware build, and a heads-up that blood pressure often moves as body weight moves, so keep your prescriber in the loop.

Do you have a thyroid condition?

Not a stop at all. It changes how I read your numbers and how fast we move.

Good that you told me. Expect a slower, steadier build with a higher protein floor, and expect the scale to be noisier week to week. That noise is not failure.

Are you in perimenopause or menopause?

One of the most commonly missed pieces in this entire conversation, and it changes the plan more than almost anything else on this page.

Then your plan gets built for this season specifically. Protein floor goes up, not down. Calcium-rich foods get made visible instead of assumed. Fibre gets spread across the day rather than dumped in one meal. And the scale gets read across weeks, because week to week it is going to be noisy and that noise is not failure.

Are you on a GLP-1 medication?

Semaglutide, tirzepatide, or any of the others. No judgment in either direction. It is a tool, and it changes what your plan has to do.

Have you had bloodwork or a genetic test?

Optional, and it makes your build sharper. Your genetics show what you tend toward. Your bloodwork shows where you are right now. Together they tell us which vitamins and minerals to build into your food on purpose.
